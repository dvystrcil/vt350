Bowden arms, walked over the bed

The solved shape of the active tool's bundle at every lock position of the lite-78 bed, from the roof exit, through the arm's loop, to the O2S lock. Design: hinge under the rear top bar (Y 490, Z 640), arm 240 mm, loop at Z 662 raised and Z 640 lowered. Gantry at today's height. It opens on the dancer: the roof exit clamps the bundle, and a weighted sheave behind the gantry holds the slack.

Dancer (static lines). The lines from the EMU to the roof are static, so the roof exit clamps the bundle. From the exit a fixed channel takes it down behind the gantry to a 20 g sheave (R 53.6) hanging on its own loop of bundle. The bundle goes back up through a second fixed channel, forward over the heads to the arm's tip, then down to the head. As the head needs more bundle, the sheave rises; as it needs less, the sheave drops. All five tools solve at every position. The sheaves travel 102–157 mm and stay above Z 116, and the channels bend no tighter than R 69. FreeCAD finds the route clear of lite-79, with thin margins of 1.1–2.6 mm. The red stretch above the lock is the same one as in the other modes (R 41 at EI 2000): the dancer doesn't cause it and doesn't fix it. Shown at EI 2000 only.

Sliding loop. The bundle runs freely through the loop and the roof-exit guide, with a 0.2 N take-up above the roof holding the slack (the study's assumption; see the note below on static lines). Every position solves; away from the lock every bend stays above R 51 and nothing drops below the lock.

Tied loop. The bundle is clamped in the loop at one cut length, so the spare length has nowhere to go. In this design it doubles back through the loop (up to 154°), bends tighter than R 51 away from the lock (R 34–48) and presses on the parked bundles. In lower-loop designs it sagged below the lock instead. None of the 30 searched designs passed tied. Shown for T0, T2 and T4 at EI 2000.

Cam stalled. The new tool's arm stays up. Every position is still reached. The red stretch just above the lock appears in every mode: the lock holds the bundle straight up and the pull bends it sideways, so it needs a bend limiter or a stiffer bundle (EI ≳ 6500).

Why the arms sit so far apart

Each arm hangs in the plane of its own bundle's roof exit, and lite-70 put the exits above the five docks. So the loops are spread as widely as the docks (X −410 to −80), even though they sit near the middle of the bed in Y. Bunching the loops around the bed's centre was checked with the same design and checks, the roof exits left where they are. Every layout passes. Bunching shortens the take-up's travel by up to about 85 mm and touches the parked bundles less when the bundle is stiff. It tightens the roof span's bend, and it doesn't change the bend at the lock. The exits stay where they are (owner, 2026-11-25).

Why a dancer: the slack stays in the chamber

The sliding loop above assumes the bundle can also slide out through the roof exit to a take-up above the roof (#244's model). It can't: the lines from the EMU to the roof are static (owner, 2026-11-25), so the roof exit clamps the bundle and every bit of spare length stays inside the chamber. The shortest bundle any position needs is about 297 mm, with the head right under its loop. The longest is about 612 mm, for T0 and T4 at the far front corner on the other side of the bed (493 mm for T2). So up to about 315 mm of slack has to be held somewhere inside.

The owner's idea is a dancer: a second, springy arm from the loop end of the main arm, reaching back toward the roof exit, whose hook presses gently down on the bundle about halfway along the roof span and may swing below the main arm. A hand estimate says a V in that 200 mm span, its point pushed 85 mm down to just above the heads, stores only about 60 mm. Holding 315 mm that way would take about 240 mm of drop, into the heads' path. Behind the gantry's rear stop (Y > 440) nothing moves, so a deeper hanging loop there is one place the slack could go. Bunching the loops toward the centre also shrinks the range. The hook needs a saddle or roller of R 51 or more, and more pull tightens the bend at the lock (roughly R ∝ 1/√pull).

The deeper loop behind the gantry is what the dancer mode above shows (vt350#318). The sheaves hang below the gantry in two stacks either side of the Z axis: T0 and T1 on the left, T2–T4 on the right, each tool in its own plane Y 446, 459 or 472. Only the legs rise through the gap behind the gantry. Most of the pull at the loop (0.27–0.37 N) is the legs' own weight, so the lightest dancer is best: a heavier one only tightens the bend at the lock. Not modelled: the channels' and sheaves' mounts, and friction in the channels.
