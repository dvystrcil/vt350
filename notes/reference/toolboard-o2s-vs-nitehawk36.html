VT350 · vt350#6 · toolboard on each Orbiter 2.5

O2S or Nitehawk-36

Both are USB toolboards that sit on the back of the Orbiter's motor, with one XT30 cable carrying 24 V and USB. They differ in four places that matter for this printer: heat, the Smart Sensor, the CAD, and work already done.

Recommendation: keep the O2S. It is the only one of the two rated past 65 °C (80 °C with a fan), which your 70 °C chamber peak needs. It is the only one with a port for the Smart Sensor. It is already in the model and the Klipper simulation.

The Nitehawk you found isn't wasted. It can run a second head until a second O2S is bought, or go on a future third tool. Running one of each means two firmware builds, two configs, and a lopsided model.

What a switch would cost

  • A new lite pass: the Nitehawk modelled from its DXF outline (the repo has no STEP and no licence), a printed mount, and the Smart Sensor re-fitted around them. Then the overlap checks and the batch-lane sweeps again.
  • The Smart Sensor's tangle and unload button need an extra input and a power feed that the Nitehawk's filament port doesn't have.
  • Redo #116's printer.cfg and the RP2040 firmware dictionaries for the simulation.
  • The 70 °C chamber peak stays unsolved: LDO rates the Nitehawk to 65 °C and gives no fan-cooled figure.

What a switch would gain: no USB serial setup, and maybe a smaller board behind the motor, which could help the parked-head clash (vt350#121). Neither board's outline has been compared yet.

Either way

  • Your motor is an LDO 36STH20; the model has a 36STH17. The model needs the real motor and the matching standoffs (18 mm for the O2S) before #121 is fixed.
  • The Smart Sensor's own temperature rating is still unknown (AC4).
